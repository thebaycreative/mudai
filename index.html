VEGETARIAN

Misir Wot

$12

A beloved Ethiopian classic, our misir wot features red lentils slowly simmered with garlic, ginger, onions, and our house-blend berbere spice. This hearty stew achieves its signature deep color and complex heat through careful layering of spices. Served on a bed of fluffy injera, it's a comfort dish that warms from the inside out.

Contains: Red lentils, berbere spice, ginger, garlic, onions, oil

Shiro

$11

Our silky shiro is made from roasted chickpea flour blended into a creamy paste with traditional spices. This dish offers a rich, nutty flavor profile that's entirely plant-based yet deeply satisfying. The smooth texture and warming spices make it a favorite among vegans and meat-eaters alike.

Ethiopian Heritage

🌾 The Origin of Teff & Injera
Ancient Ethiopia
Injera, the foundation of Ethiopian cuisine, is made from teff flour—a grain native to Ethiopia that dates back thousands of years. This nutrient-rich ancient grain is fermented into a spongy sourdough flatbread that serves as both plate and utensil, embodying the communal spirit of Ethiopian dining.
☕ The Coffee Legacy
9th Century
Legend tells of a goat herder named Kaldi who discovered coffee in the Ethiopian highlands. The bean's journey from these misty mountains to tables worldwide began centuries ago, and the traditional Ethiopian coffee ceremony remains a sacred ritual of hospitality and connection.
👥 Community & Injera
Timeless Tradition
In Ethiopian culture, eating is an act of community. The custom of "gursha"—hand-feeding loved ones as a sign of affection and respect—transforms every meal into a shared celebration. At Mudai, we honor this tradition by creating space for your family and friends to gather, connect, and enjoy authentic flavors together.

The Ethiopian Coffee Ceremony

①

Roasting

Fresh green coffee beans are roasted over hot coals, filling the air with an aromatic, earthy scent that invites guests to share this sacred ritual.

②

Grinding

The roasted beans are ground by hand using a traditional mortar and pestle, releasing their full flavor and creating a meditative moment of connection.

③

Brewing

The grounds are steeped in a traditional jebena pot with hot water, then poured into small handleless cups and shared among all present.
